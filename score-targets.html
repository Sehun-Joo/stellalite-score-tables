Stellalite: difficulty ranges first, popularity second

Membership comes from the official Satellite and Stella tables, fetched 26 November 2026. Score and total-play data come from the 29 May 2026 LR2IR archive.

5,031 current charts → 4,548 modeled charts, 20,442 players, 2,730,428 comparable personal bests. 483 charts lacked archive coverage or sufficient comparable scores. See all exclusions and reasons. Counts are for charts, so different charts of one song can appear.

Model and evidence

One PB per player/chart; exclude zero/invalid/flagged scores, flagged users, assist options and nonmodal maximum scores. Players need at least 10 extracted charts; retained charts need at least 30 usable PBs after that player filter. A robust regularized full-score model estimates player ability and chart response. Separate regularized logistic fits estimate each target's 50% historical-PB achievement threshold. The three targets are AAA = 8/9, MAX− = 17/18 and 98% = 98/100, tested with exact integer arithmetic.

The scale is fitted separately to this Stella + Satellite cohort; its numbers should not be compared directly with the Insane tables. Popularity is not used to fit difficulty. Practice exposure, old scores and personal strengths remain confounders; predictions are not next-attempt success probabilities. Provisional means fewer than 100 PBs, fewer than five successes or misses, or fewer than 30 players within one ability unit of the fitted threshold.

Predetermined cuts and selection

For each target, first calculate every boundary from the full eligible difficulty distribution and save it in range-cuts.json. Use the same progression rule as the finer Insane edition: 0.155 ability-unit base steps; snap a boundary within ±20% of its step toward the largest nearby adjacent-score gap of at least 0.01. Above the full pool's 90th percentile, widen steps smoothly by a factor of 1 + 0.6 × (nominal lower boundary − tail start). Empty ranges are omitted. This is a practice-design heuristic, not a learned human training curve. Bounds include the lower value and exclude the upper value; displayed bounds are rounded.

Only after fixing those ranges, sort each range by total archived chart play_count descending and keep its first 20 charts. Ties use unique-player count descending, then MD5. Never move boundaries or borrow charts to reach 20. Display and export follow the same popularity order within each difficulty range. Each target provides all-rankings.json with the entire modeled pool and selection flags, and rankings.json with selected charts.

Held-out-player validation

80% of players train the chart models. For held-out players, half their records estimate ability and half are evaluated, requiring at least five reference charts. The near-target subset uses training-only score-model parameters. Scores below are log loss; lower is better. Evaluation covers the eligible pool before popularity selection. This is a fixed split, not a guarantee of future performance.
